GOOGLE DORKS COMMANDS
=====================

Every Google search operator, what it does, and whether it still works.
Last checked: November 2026.

Quick rules before you start:
  - No space after the colon.      site:example.com   not   site: example.com
  - OR must be in capitals.        pdf OR docx        not   pdf or docx
  - No space after the minus.      -draft             not   - draft
  - Use brackets to group.         (admin OR login)


-----------------------------------------------------------------
DOCUMENTED BY GOOGLE
These are officially supported and the safest to rely on.
-----------------------------------------------------------------

"..."  (quotes)
  Matches the exact phrase, in that order.
  Example:  "internal use only"
  Note:     Also stops Google swapping your word for a synonym.

site:
  Limits results to one website, subdomain or folder.
  Example:  site:example.com
            site:blog.example.com
            site:example.com/docs
            site:*.example.com
  Note:     Works with whole TLDs too, e.g. site:gov.uk

-  (minus)
  Leaves out results containing a word, or from a site.
  Example:  privacy policy -template
            site:example.com -site:www.example.com
  Note:     Can be put in front of other operators, e.g. -inurl:blog

filetype:
  Returns only files with that extension.
  Example:  filetype:pdf
            site:example.com filetype:xlsx
  Note:     Returns a sample, not every file. Works with pdf, doc,
            docx, xls, xlsx, ppt, pptx, txt, csv, log, xml and more.

after:
  Pages Google thinks were updated on or after a date.
  Example:  after:2024-01-01
            after:2025
  Note:     Formats: YYYY, YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD.

before:
  Pages Google thinks were updated on or before a date.
  Example:  before:2023-06-30
  Note:     Combine with after: for a date range.
            Dates are Google's best guess and can be wrong.


-----------------------------------------------------------------
UNDOCUMENTED, BUT THEY WORK
Google doesn't officially support these. They work today but
could change without warning.
-----------------------------------------------------------------

intitle:
  The word appears in the page title.
  Example:  intitle:"index of"
            intitle:login

allintitle:
  Every following word appears in the title.
  Example:  allintitle:admin login panel
  Note:     Doesn't mix well with other operators. Use several
            intitle: instead if you need to combine.

inurl:
  The word appears somewhere in the URL.
  Example:  inurl:admin
            site:example.com inurl:dev

allinurl:
  Every following word appears in the URL.
  Example:  allinurl:wp-content uploads

intext:
  The word appears in the body of the page.
  Example:  intext:"powered by wordpress"

allintext:
  Every following word appears in the body.
  Example:  allintext:username password login

ext:
  Same as filetype:.
  Example:  ext:log
            site:example.com ext:sql

OR  /  |
  Matches either term.
  Example:  ext:sql OR ext:bak
            ext:sql | ext:bak
  Note:     OR must be uppercase. Wrap in brackets when combining.

*  (wildcard)
  Stands in for one or more words inside a quoted phrase.
  Example:  "how to * a linux server"
            "default * password"

..  (number range)
  Matches any number in a range.
  Example:  "annual report" 2018..2024
            camera $200..$500


-----------------------------------------------------------------
HIT AND MISS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

AROUND(n)
  Two words within n words of each other.
  Example:  password AROUND(3) reset
  Status:   Never documented. Sometimes works, often ignored.

info:
  Shows what Google has on a URL.
  Example:  info:example.com
  Status:   Unreliable since 2017. Usually just returns the page.

related:
  Finds sites similar to the one given.
  Example:  related:example.com
  Status:   Being phased out since July 2023.


-----------------------------------------------------------------
GONE - DON'T USE THESE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

cache:
  Used to show Google's saved copy of a page.
  Removed:  September 2024
  Instead:  Wayback Machine - https://web.archive.org/

link:
  Used to show pages linking to a URL.
  Removed:  2017
  Instead:  Google Search Console, or a backlink tool.

+  (plus)
  Used to force an exact word.
  Removed:  October 2011
  Instead:  Put the word in quotes.

inanchor: / allinanchor:
  Used to match link text pointing at a page.
  Status:   Results are now so thin it's not worth using.


-----------------------------------------------------------------
PUTTING THEM TOGETHER
-----------------------------------------------------------------

  site:example.com filetype:pdf "annual report" -draft after:2024

  = PDFs on example.com
    containing the exact phrase "annual report"
    not containing "draft"
    updated since 2024


-----------------------------------------------------------------
THINGS TO KNOW
-----------------------------------------------------------------

  - Result counts on site: searches are rough estimates.
  - Google caps a query at 32 words. Operators count.
  - Run too many searches too fast and you'll get a CAPTCHA.
  - Bing and DuckDuckGo support site: and filetype:, but not much
    else. It's worth trying more than one engine.
